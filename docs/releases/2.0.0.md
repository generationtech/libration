# Libration 2.0.0 — Planetary Scene & Live Earth

Libration 2.0.0 is a major expansion of the product since [v1.1.0](https://github.com/generationtech/libration/releases/tag/v1.1.0).

1.1.0 refined the unified time model and chrome instrument. 2.0.0 keeps that instrument and turns the world beneath it into a planetary scene: composable maps and illumination, a full eclipse system, space objects, and honest live-Earth observation.

Libration remains a longitude-first world time instrument. One canonical UTC instant still drives every frame. Display modes still format time; they do not mutate it. What has changed is the breadth of the scene that instant can describe.

This is a **source release**. Run it as a browser application. The Tauri desktop shell is present but not load-bearing.

---

## Highlights

- **Scene architecture.** Scene content is an ordered, configurable stack (`SceneConfig` / Layers), not a handful of hard-wired overlays.
- **Maps.** Eleven curated base-map families, including topography, bathymetry, land cover, climate, and population, with per-family brightness, contrast, gamma, and saturation.
- **Illumination.** Continuous solar twilight, moonlight-aware night, and Black Marble night lights composed upstream into one planetary raster.
- **Eclipse system.** Offline NASA/Espenak–Meeus solar and lunar eclipses: forecast geography, live footprints, reference-city circumstances, and alignment presentation.
- **Astronomy & space objects.** Planetary subpoints, tracks, and loci; lunar ground track, locus, and optical libration; Milky Way galactic presentation and viewing events; live ISS visualization.
- **Live Earth.** USGS earthquakes with provenance, filters, and hover labels; current-observation global Clouds from multiple geostationary IR sources.
- **Event playback.** One chronological Demo timeline for solar eclipses, lunar eclipses, and Milky Way viewing windows.

## Eclipse system

Solar and lunar eclipses use a bundled NASA/Espenak–Meeus authority for 1900–2100. Event truth is offline. Visualization-grade Sun and Moon models do not invent eclipse geography.

**Solar eclipses**

- Live umbra / antumbra / partial footprint at the current product instant
- Forecast corridor for upcoming events, with a configurable horizon (factory 7 days)
- Continuous local obscuration shading in the illumination raster during an active eclipse
- Live alignment / beam when a terrestrial central intersection exists
- Restrained map labels and a dismissible event-information panel

**Lunar eclipses**

- Active Earth-shadow on the Moon glyph
- Moonlight attenuated by actual disc coverage
- Event-static visibility footprint: one closed line for every location from which some part of the eclipse is geometrically visible during the event
- Separate lunar forecast horizon on the same eclipse service

**Reference-city circumstances**

Local contacts, magnitude, and visibility for the chrome reference city. Global eclipse geography is never filtered by that city. Copy says when an event is not visible locally; it never pretends the global event is absent.

Eclipse presentation is independently configurable (type filters, labels, appearance, forecast horizons). Factory solar and lunar masters are on; events appear only when they are relevant.

## Astronomy & space objects

**Moon.** Optical libration on the Moon glyph (ring or crosshair), observer-oriented by default to the reference city. Optional lunar ground track (past and future independently styled) and a compact lunar locus.

**Planets.** Mercury–Neptune plus Pluto as terrestrial subpoints from a bundled offline ephemeris. Optional ground tracks and daily same-clock planetary loci. These follow product time; they are not live internet feeds.

**Milky Way.** Galactic-plane zenith ribbon, approximate band, sparse ribs, and Galactic-center marker. Optional Galactic-center altitude contours. Reference-city viewing windows when the Galactic center is favorably elevated under astronomical darkness and low modeled moonlight, with a static peak-UTC viewing footprint. This is geometry and a viewing opportunity, not a weather or light-pollution forecast.

**ISS.** Live visualization from a current TLE (CelesTrak, with failover). The marker is the SGP4 position at the product instant. Configurable orbit track, past/future horizons, glyph, and label. Provenance and TLE freshness are honest: live, degraded, or hidden. Fixture TLE is never painted as live. Historical Demo hides the overlay.

Planets, Milky Way, and ISS live under Layers → Space objects.

## Live Earth data

Live observational layers require network access. They stay off by default. They never fetch on the render path. When product time is not close to wall-clock now, current-only layers hide rather than invent history.

**Earthquakes**

- Live USGS all-day feed
- Honest loading / live / stale / unavailable status
- Local filters: minimum magnitude (factory 2.5+), maximum age (factory 24 hours), earthquakes-only
- Compact persistent labels (factory 4.0+) and optional hover labels (`M4.6 · place`)

Earthquakes are a current snapshot. Historical earthquake playback is not part of this release.

**ISS** is documented under Astronomy & space objects above. It is a live current-only feed with the same live-enough product-time gate.

## Weather & Clouds

Clouds is a current-observation global IR overlay, not a weather-forecast product and not a physical illumination layer.

What 2.0.0 ships:

- Near-current global cloud depiction from multiple geostationary satellite sources (GOES-East, GOES-West, Himawari, Meteosat, plus a global geostationary-ring backstop)
- Each source keeps its own observation time; the freshest valid observations are composed independently
- Coverage and viewing-quality inform which source owns each location
- Observation-age and provenance appear in Layers → Weather (including mixed freshness when sources differ)
- Failed live acquisition does not silently fall back to fixture imagery
- Historical Demo hides Clouds; the enablement checkbox stays on, and returning to current time restores the overlay
- Clouds remain informational. They do not darken or relight the planetary illumination raster

This is a mature IR cloud foundation. Remaining visual differences at ordinary scale are inherent IR and provider limits, not unfinished composition work.

## Maps & visual presentation

Eleven bundled substrate families:

- Natural-colour / Blue Marble (including month-aware families)
- Topography
- Political
- Geology
- Bathymetry
- Land cover
- Climate
- Population

Per-family brightness, contrast, gamma, and saturation persist with the selected map. Overlay readability adapts to the substrate. Illumination composes day, continuous twilight, moonlight, night lights, and active solar-eclipse obscuration into one upstream raster. Polar day and polar night follow real solar geometry.

Configuration is a six-tab panel (`C`). Layers, Chrome, and Data use topic subpanels so related controls stay grouped without changing what is persisted.

## Time, Demo & event playback

The 1.1.0 unified time model is unchanged in spirit: one canonical UTC instant per frame; display modes format; they do not move the tape or mutate product time.

**Demo** remains a single shared clock. **Event playback** (Data → Event playback) sequences bundled astronomical events through that clock:

- Solar eclipses, lunar eclipses, and Milky Way viewing windows in one chronological stream
- Start, Next, Previous, Reset, Stop, and Loop
- Loop wraps to the earliest event in range; Loop off pauses at the end
- Layers still own what is drawn; Data owns when it is viewed

Weather and earthquake events are not part of playback.

## Architecture / reliability

Durable points that matter to this release:

- `RenderPlan` remains the rendering boundary. Backends do not decide product behaviour.
- Product time is unified. Live observational data distinguish product time, observation time, and acquisition time.
- Dynamic sources expose honest provenance and freshness instead of presenting stale or fixture data as live.
- DEV visual-scenario machinery (`?scenario=`, Clouds diagnostics, and related development-only strings) is excluded from production builds.
- The application remains browser-first. Maps, fonts, eclipse catalogs, and planetary/Milky Way geometry are bundled and work offline. Live Clouds, earthquakes, and ISS require the network.

## Known limitations / future work

These are honest bounds of 2.0.0, not defects in the shipped work.

- Clouds are IR-derived and current-only. There is no historical cloud TIME query and no weather-event playback.
- Warm/low cloud and cold-surface / polar ice remain inherent infrared ambiguities.
- Polar geostationary coverage gaps stay transparent. They are not treated as clear sky.
- Live observational layers require network access and hide when product time is not current.
- The Tauri desktop shell is not a release-critical path. Source / browser remains the primary delivery.
- Visible/GeoColor clouds, radar, wind, lightning, precipitation, and related weather products are not part of 2.0.0.
- Earthquake clustering, depth symbology, click-to-detail, and historical earthquake playback are not part of 2.0.0.
- A standing ambient lunar-visibility overlay, aggregated Milky Way observing-quality forecasts, and additional spacecraft remain future work.
